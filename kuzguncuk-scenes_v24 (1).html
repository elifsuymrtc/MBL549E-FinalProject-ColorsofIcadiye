and pedestrian density along İcadiye Caddesi
Intended Audience
Architects and urban designers — including those without a data background — alongside planners, spatial researchers, and environmental psychologists interested in how built colour shapes human behaviour and spatial preference. Visualizations are layered: patterns read at a glance, precise metrics on hover.
Key Narrative
Does façade colour diversity drive pedestrian engagement? Using Google Places review counts and Street View imagery as proxies, this study treats İcadiye Caddesi as a dataset — its buildings as data points.
Reflection
Colourful facades do align with higher activity zones — but the most active point sits next to the Kuzguncuk ferry pier. Transit proximity is a confounding factor: colour enriches experience, but connectivity likely drives density.

Research Context
As contemporary cities trend toward standardized building typologies, neighborhoods with a distinct architectural identity become increasingly rare. Kuzguncuk, one of Istanbul's most characteristically preserved historic districts, offers a unique case where facade diversity, human scale, and spatial memory coexist within a rapidly transforming metropolis.
This study examines how site-specific facade qualities along İcadiye Street relate to pedestrian density, providing evidence that architectural character is not merely aesthetic; it is a measurable driver of human behaviour and spatial preference.
